# Brief and brand

## Define the decision

Start from the current brief, supplied files and brand website. Identify what the client must decide after reading: positioning, audiences, investment, a launch approach, partners or a commercial scope. Keep the objective slide short; move diagnosis into the relevant chapter.

Capture these fields in the brief map:

| Field | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Business outcome | Revenue, qualified acquisition, awareness, consideration or another explicit outcome |
| Communication task | What the audience should know, feel or do |
| Brand and product | Exact names, proposition, proof, range and availability |
| Geography | Country, cities, languages and market priorities |
| Audiences | Consumer, trade, buyer, influencer and decision roles |
| Budget | Currency, scenarios, net/gross basis, fees, tax and production inclusion |
| Timing | Campaign window, launch date, seasons and operational dependencies |
| Constraints | Mandatory or excluded channels, properties, claims and commercial commitments |
| Deliverable | Format, slide count, audience, template, supporting model and exports |
| Success | Primary outcome, definitions, available baseline and reporting source |
| Existing work | Current deck, approved copy, exclusions and revision scope |

Distinguish objectives from tactics: a request for video is an input; the change video must create is the task. Distinguish ambition from a forecast. A requested frequency level is something to model, not an observed outcome.

Ask only for information that materially changes the strategy or prevents production. Group essential questions. Continue independent research while answers are pending. When the user authorizes reasonable assumptions, record them and finish the proposal. If no budget is supplied, show allocation logic or explicitly illustrative scenarios; do not invent a client commitment. If timing is unknown, use phases with entry conditions rather than fabricated dates.

## Audit the brand

Read relevant owned pages: home, products/services, about, proof/case studies, distribution, pricing where public, FAQs and conversion path. Record the URL and observation date. Map:

- Product benefits, specifications, distinctive features and credible proof.
- Brand voice, visual identity, exact naming, logos, product artwork and asset rights.
- Positioning, segment, price cues and alternatives considered by buyers.
- Where the product is actually available and what the user must do to buy or enquire.
- Gaps between the promise, content, landing page and sales journey.

Treat owned claims as brand statements unless independently substantiated. A website saying 'market leader' is not a market-share study. Do not fabricate customer stories, credentials, awards or access to a proprietary audience.

## Maintain the current version

Prefer the latest explicit instruction and approved working deck. Keep a revision register containing slide IDs, locked copy/order, requested edits, exclusions and dependent slides. Do not retrieve discarded copy from older files merely because it is convenient. Do not universalize one brief's white background, screenshot crop, partner, event or persona.

For visual-only work, extract and lock the original text, slide order, numbers, sources and approved strategic choices before editing. Change content only when the user authorizes it. Preserve notes and reference context where possible; report a substantive factual issue separately if correction is outside scope.
