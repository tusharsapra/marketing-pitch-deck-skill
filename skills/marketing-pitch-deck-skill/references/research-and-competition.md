# Research and competition

## Plan research around decisions

List the questions that could change the recommendation. For each, define the evidence required, source, geography, period and likely slide use. Research only the relevant category, audiences, journey and market; do not fill the deck with unrelated global statistics.

Prefer original official datasets, studies with a readable method, first-party product/platform documents and directly observed advertisements. Use credible secondary reporting to locate the original or fill a clearly labelled gap. Read the underlying page or report rather than relying on search snippets. Capture publication date separately from data period. Check definitions, denominators, market scope, sample and limitations.

Maintain source and claim IDs. Use these evidence classes consistently:

| Class | Meaning | Presentation treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| verified | Accessible evidence supports the specific statement within its stated scope | Source and scoped wording |
| supplied | Client brief, export or proposal provides the information | Attribute to supplied input; keep its limitations |
| directional | A proxy, partial observation or model informs a hypothesis | Describe the proxy and uncertainty |
| assumption | A planning input lacks direct validation | Label on the affected slide/model |
| proposed | A future idea, property, inventory or action is being recommended | Label as proposed; state confirmation dependencies |

Never convert a plausible observation into a verified fact. Keep material uncertainty visible near the decision; appendix detail alone does not cure a misleading headline. A source ledger verifies provenance, not the truth of every interpretation.

## Market and demand

Connect market context to the actual brief: city opportunity, seasonal window, category adoption, buying barriers or portfolio priority. Separate market size, category sales, household penetration, search activity and media audiences. They answer different questions and use different units.

For Google Trends or similar indexed data, preserve queries/topics, search type, category, geography, date range and comparison panel. Compare within equivalent settings. Trends is normalized relative interest; it is not absolute volume, market share, recall, spend or revenue. A zero can mean insufficient data. Separately normalized charts cannot be compared as if they share an absolute scale. Treat spikes as leads to investigate, not proof of campaign causality.

Use keyword volumes/CPCs only from an accessible source or supplied export and label estimates and settings. If account tools are unavailable, use a scoped qualitative demand assessment. Do not invent precision to complete a table.

## Competition and communication

Select direct competitors and relevant alternatives; explain why an adjacent category is useful when included. Inspect the brand's own communication too. For each observable ad or journey, capture:

- Advertiser, exact product, market, observation date, source URL and original asset.
- Platform, visible placement and format; distinguish observation from suggested future inventory.
- Message, hook, benefit, offer, proof, tone and call to action.
- Destination and visible path from attention to enquiry/purchase.
- Repeated patterns, meaningful differences and the implication for this brand.

Use public ad libraries, search observations, owned channels and authorized exports where available. Discover their current supported access; do not assume unrestricted APIs or complete coverage. An observed ad does not reveal spend, bidding, targeting, results or a full media strategy unless the source actually supplies them. Ad-library absence does not prove inactivity.

Pair claims with relevant, readable evidence screenshots. Retain originals and source context. Use a crop or full screenshot according to the current brief and the analytical purpose. Do not place tiny screenshots as decoration. Compare strategically useful patterns rather than counting ads without coverage context.

## Research handoff

Produce a concise evidence summary: finding, evidence, scope/limitation, implication and affected decision. Every priority recommendation should be traceable to a source, supplied requirement or transparent planning hypothesis. If evidence is missing, say what would validate it and continue with the appropriate uncertainty level.
