# Audience and strategy

## Build cohorts before selecting platforms

Define cohorts by needs and decision roles. Use an audience map with role, trigger, desired outcome, barrier, required proof, message, geography, journey stage, reachable signals and desired action. Distinguish end users from buyers, recommenders, installers, specifiers or assisted-sales teams where they have different jobs.

Include location beside cohort profiling when location changes access, language, distribution or demand. Keep audience profiling separate from execution detail so the reader first understands whom the plan serves. Avoid unsupported demographic precision or decorative personas with invented quotes. Label inferred barriers as hypotheses until supported.

Translate cohorts into feasible buying signals. A strategic cohort is not automatically a selectable platform segment. List what can actually be bought or observed: geography, context, first-party list, intent, interest proxy or partner environment. State where precision is uncertain. Premium-device use, residential context or financial content can be directional affluence signals; do not present them as verified socioeconomic classification.

## Map the journey

Connect the trigger, discovery, evaluation, proof, action and fulfilment. Identify what must happen on the landing page or in sales for the media action to become a qualified outcome. Separate search demand capture from demand creation. For assisted conversions, define lead qualification, routing, response time, sales capacity and offline outcome feedback.

## Synthesize the strategy

Write a compact thesis containing:

1. The commercially relevant audience tension or category problem.
2. The brand's credible right to address it, supported by product truth.
3. The change in belief or behavior the plan will create.
4. The strategic choice, including priority and tradeoff.
5. The evidence or assumption supporting that choice.

Avoid deriving the strategy solely from a clever campaign phrase. Research must lead to choices. A differentiating claim requires evidence; a unique-looking execution is not automatically a differentiated proposition.

Maintain a decision trace:

| Evidence or input | Interpretation | Choice | Execution | Success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scoped finding or requirement | What it means for this audience | What changes in the plan | Where and how it happens | Defined metric and decision |

Challenge priority choices: why this audience, why this environment, why this idea, why now, and what is deprioritized? If changing the brand name leaves the strategy equally convincing, make the product truth, barrier or choice more specific.
